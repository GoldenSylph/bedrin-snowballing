# Snowballing Literature Review — Instruction
**Method:** Wohlin (2014) | **Databases:** Google Scholar · WoS · Scopus

---

## Research Question
> *Does decentralization in digital transformation impact sustainability as stable long-term development?*

A paper must address at least **two** of three core concepts:
1. **Decentralization** — blockchain, DLT, distributed systems, DAOs
2. **Digital Transformation** — organizational, institutional, or societal
3. **Sustainability as stable development** — institutional resilience, economic stability, long-term governance *(not ecological)*

---

## Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria

**Include if ALL true:**
- Peer-reviewed journal
- Addresses ≥2 core concepts
- Published after 2015, in English
- Full text accessible

**Exclude if ANY true:**
- Sustainability framed purely in ecological/environmental terms
- Conference paper or grey literature only
- Duplicate already in tracker
- Full text inaccessible

---

## Phase 1 — Verify Seeds
For each seed confirm three things before starting:
1. **Indexing** — check WoS ([mjl.clarivate.com](https://mjl.clarivate.com)), Scopus, DOAJ, or ERIH PLUS. Record all confirmed indexes.
2. **Free access** — confirm PDF is openable via publisher, Unpaywall, ResearchGate, or library VPN.
3. **Relevance** — read abstract and confirm sustainability is framed as *stability*, not ecology.

Mark passing seeds as **Included: Yes / Round: Seed / Direction: —** in tracker.

---

## Phase 2 — Backward Snowballing (Round 1)
1. Open each seed and scan its reference list.
2. For each relevant-looking title, add a tracker row: **Round 1 / Backward / Pending**.
3. Note which seed it came from.
4. Log all candidates first — do not screen yet.

> Expected: 40–80 Pending rows after all 5 seeds.

---

## Phase 3 — Screen Backward Candidates
Filter tracker by **Pending**. For each paper, read title and abstract only:
- **Passes** → Included: Yes, Relevance: High/Medium/Low, one-sentence note on why
- **Fails** → Included: No, one-sentence note on which criterion failed

> Expected: 15–25 included from 40–80 candidates.

---

## Phase 4 — Forward Snowballing (Round 1)
1. Search each seed title in Google Scholar.
2. Click **Cited by** and scan the citing papers list.
3. Add relevant ones: **Round 1 / Forward / Pending**.
4. Screen using same process as Phase 3.

> ⚠️ Google Scholar has no public API. Retrieve the "Cited by" list manually then paste it for AI-assisted screening.

---

## Phase 5 — Round 2 Snowballing
1. Filter tracker by **Round 1 / Included: Yes** — treat these as new seeds.
2. Repeat Phases 2–4 for each one, logging as **Round 2**.
3. **Saturation check:** stop when new papers found are already in your tracker.

> Most reviews saturate by Round 2 or 3.

---

## Phase 6 — Full-Text Reading
Filter by **Included: Yes** across all rounds. Read each paper fully and update the Notes field with 1–2 sentences on its argument relevant to your research question.

---

## Phase 7 — Extract Numbers & Write Methodology

Use tracker filters to get the numbers for your methodology paragraph:

| Filter | Gives you |
|---|---|
| All rows | Total candidates screened |
| Included: Yes | Final sample size |
| Direction: Backward | From reference lists |
| Direction: Forward | From citation searches |
| Round: 1, 2, 3… | Papers per round |
| By index (SSCI, Scopus…) | Quality breakdown |

**Template:**
> *"Following Wohlin (2014), snowballing was conducted from 5 seed papers across [N] rounds. Round 1 identified [X] backward and [Y] forward candidates, of which [Z] were retained. Round 2 produced [X] additional candidates, of which [Y] were retained. Saturation was reached after Round [N]. The final sample comprised [N] papers indexed across [databases]."*
